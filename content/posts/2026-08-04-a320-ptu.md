If you've sat in an A320 during pushback, while a tug pushes the aircraft back
from the gate, you've probably heard something barking. That is the power
transfer unit.

Lose an engine and you lose the hydraulic system its pump drove, along with
everything running on it. So two of the A320's hydraulic systems, green and
yellow, are joined by one shaft through a sealed bulkhead, with a bent-axis
piston unit at each end. Whichever system holds the higher pressure turns its
unit as a motor and drives the other as a pump, so green can feed yellow or
yellow can feed green. No fluid crosses.

Both systems run at 3000 psi. When the difference between them exceeds about
500 psi, the shaft breaks away and spins up, pressurizes the low side until the
difference collapses, and drops out. A second later the difference crosses the
threshold again. That cycling is the barking you hear from your seat during
pushback, when only one engine is turning.

I built [the barking dog](/sim/a320-ptu/) to watch that cycle run. The 500 psi
threshold is the aircraft's. The flow rates and time constants are mine, and I
slowed them down so you can watch one cycle. Drop an engine pump offline, drag
the flight-control demand, and watch the pressure difference cross 500 psi.
Turn the sound on.
