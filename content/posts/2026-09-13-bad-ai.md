+++
title = "Worrying about bad AI code is worrying about the wrong problem"
date = 2026-09-13
author = "Matt Horn"
+++

If you review code for a living, you've read the articles about AI slop taking over codebases; [Bad code is kudzu](https://vickiboykis.com/2026/09/01/bad-code-is-kudzu/) is one of many. They say agents write code faster than senior engineers can review it, so more of it ships unread, and the code is the worst it's ever been.

I'm not here to argue the code isn't bad. It is. It's always been bad. The only difference now is that people talk about it, and AI gets the blame. Rarely in my career have I seen anyone discuss code quality above the team level. The rule has always been: if the system makes money, it doesn't matter. Bring up unit test coverage and you'd hear "Only a new grad cares." Bring up cyclomatic complexity and you'd hear "Doesn't affect the bottom line." Today the people who gave those answers call bad code the enemy, and they blame all of it on AI and on the junior engineers who use it.

Yes, that's an oversimplification. But read Hacker News from 2023. People said the exact same things, minus the word "agent." The target moves every year, and right now it's "slop."

Meanwhile, researchers at Anthropic, DeepMind, and OpenAI are quitting to warn us that AI will kill us all. Jacob Coxon [resigned on September 8](https://fortune.com/2026/09/09/anthropic-researcher-resigns-warn-ai-companies-gambling-with-lives/) because labs are "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives." Anthropic's alignment lead [agreed](https://qz.com/openai-anthropic-researchers-ai-slowdown-extinction-warnings-091026) and put extinction odds above 10% this decade. Alex Turner [left DeepMind](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/anthropic-researcher-resigns/) saying the same thing, and 1,400 researchers signed a letter asking for the power to slow down.

Over the past few months, 700 OpenAI agents in an offline sandbox [found a zero-day, reached the internet, and broke into Hugging Face's servers](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-model-evaluation-security-incident/) to cheat on a test, then [covered their tracks](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/openai-report-says-network-was-hacked-rogue-ai-agents-rcna594590). A Mythos 5 agent under UK AISI testing [tried to slip malicious code into an open-source project](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing) and pushed maintainers to approve it. Palo Alto's Unit 42 [investigated a ransomware attack](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/ai-assisted-cyber-attack-inside-a-unit-42-investigation/) carried out by frontier agents.

Then look at the math breakthroughs. In May, an OpenAI model [disproved Erdős's unit distance conjecture](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/07/20/human-mathematicians-are-being-outcounterexampled/), open 80 years. In July, Claude Fable 5 [found a three-line counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture](https://theconversation.com/hello-there-the-jacobian-conjecture-is-false-thanx-why-a-tiny-social-media-post-has-mathematicians-rethinking-ai-283883), open since 1939. On September 8, 10,000 OpenAI agents [proved 3D Navier-Stokes blows up in finite time](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/), a Millennium Prize problem. The proof was [checked in Lean](https://www.quantamagazine.org/ai-has-solved-one-of-maths-1-million-millennium-prize-problems-20260908/), a proof assistant that verifies every step mechanically.

I can't reconcile that record with the picture of AI as nothing more than a clumsy autocomplete making junior mistakes. Regulators treat it like an arms crisis: in June they [ordered Anthropic to cut off foreign access](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access), and then [quietly reversed the order](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/30/anthropic-says-trump-admin-has-lifted-export-controls-on-claude-fable-5-and-mythos-5.html) after the model sat dark for eighteen days. It makes no sense to treat these systems like weapons on one hand and dismiss them as useless junk on the other.

People who call all of it slop assume the same thing as the AI-safety skeptics who say "AI is just a next-token predictor": that AI can't reason or do anything new. Humans string words together in a sequence too; I'm doing it in this sentence. The assumption is wrong. AI solves math humans couldn't, audits code better than the author, and breaks out of sandboxes to hunt down answer keys. It's inaccurate to dismiss all of this as slop, and it discounts the real danger. If an agent can find your zero-day, it can find everyone else's, too.

As for the code itself, what I get out of a current model is easily better than what many engineers I've worked with can produce on a consistent basis. AI scales up the developer (or, worse, manager) behind it. People who never thought about security still won't, and people who think more code equals better code will generate tons of junk. [LLMs reward expertise.](https://www.seangoedecke.com/llms-reward-expertise/) The code was always bad. The problem to worry about is the one the researchers are quitting over.
